\section{Introduction}\label{sec1}

Extratropical cyclones are key components of the midlatitude storm tracks and major drivers of synoptic variability, acting as transient eddies that transport heat, moisture, and momentum poleward. By organizing fronts and modulating the occurrence of precipitation and wind extremes, they exert a strong influence on regional climates and on the large-scale atmospheric energy cycle. Understanding the dynamical processes that control cyclone growth and intensification is therefore essential for interpreting climate variability and for improving numerical models used in weather prediction and climate projections.

Over South America, three cyclogenesis hotspots have been identified in the literature: eastern Argentina (ARG), the La Plata River basin (LA-PLATA), and southeastern Brazil (SE-BR) \citep{gan1991surface,hoskins2005new,gramcianinov2019properties,crespo2021potential}. Cyclones forming in these regions often undergo genesis, intensification, and maturity over or near the continent \citep{couto2024new}, substantially modulating weather across subtropical and extratropical South America and being associated with high-impact hazards such as extreme rainfall \citep[e.g.,][]{de2021ocean,de2024extreme}, intense winds \citep{cardoso2022synoptic,de2021ocean}, high sea waves \citep{da2025classification,gramcianinov2023impact}, and storm surges along coastal areas \citep{Leal2026WeatherPatterns,tecchio2024mean}. These events translate into socioeconomic losses and disruptions to local infrastructure \citep[e.g.][]{da2022climate,marengo2024impactos}, which is particularly critical given the presence of major metropolitan areas (e.g., São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, and Buenos Aires) and strategic coastal assets such as the Port of Santos, the largest in Latin America.

The cyclone phase space (CPS) framework, which diagnoses cyclone thermal-core structure (warm vs. cold) and symmetry \citep{hart2003cyclone}, is among the most widely used objective tools for classifying cyclone types and phase transitions \citep{wood2023phase}. However, CPS is fundamentally a structural diagnostic: it tracks how the storm’s thermal and symmetry signatures evolve, but it does not explicitly diagnose the physical processes and environmental controls driving those changes (e.g., vertical shear, baroclinicity, air–sea fluxes, or PV interactions). Moreover, CPS-based classification can be ambiguous for certain hybrid cyclone types. For instance, warm seclusions can exhibit warm, relatively symmetric cores \citep[e.g.,][]{dekker2018characteristics}, yielding CPS signatures that may overlap those of tropical(-like) cyclones. In addition, some systems follow atypical CPS pathways—for example, extratropical transitions in which the core becomes cold before a marked increase in thermal asymmetry \citep{kitabatake2011climatology}. Finally, because CPS magnitudes and commonly used thresholds depend on storm size, tropospheric depth, and regional atmospheric structure, several studies have proposed adaptations of CPS parameters (e.g., analysis radius and/or pressure layers) for medicanes, Arctic cyclones/polar lows, and South Atlantic subtropical cyclones \citep{gozzo2014subtropical,comellas2021evaluation,croad2023role,saraceni2023mediterranean,miglietta2025defining}.

Some authors proposed objective cyclone classification methods based on environmental parameters \citep[e.g][]{yanase2014parameter,emanuel2025cyclops}. For instance, \citet{corner2025classification} clustered extratropical cyclones cyclone into four intensity types, that differ in terms of not only intensity but on life cycle characteristics (e.g., duration and deepening rate) and on geographical location. Meanwhile, \citet{graf2017objective} attempted to classify cyclogenesis events using over 30 environmental variables. They found that these systems represent a continuum between systems with low/high level forcing with varying importance of diabatic processes. Noteworthy, \citet{han2025system} used a machine learning approach for classifying cyclone types, although, for this method it is required previously classified systems, which can propagate biases. Here, we instead adopt a phase-resolved energetics framework, quantifying the relative contributions of baroclinic, barotropic and diabatic pathways throughout the cyclone life cycle. By focusing on energy conversions rather than solely on environmental proxies or peak intensity, our approach provides a process-based classification that distinguishes cyclones with similar intensity or background conditions but fundamentally different growth mechanisms.

Extratropical cyclone development is traditionally framed in terms of baroclinic instability, and the canonical eddy life cycle is often summarized as a phase of baroclinic growth followed by barotropic decay — i.e., a net transfer of eddy kinetic energy back to the mean flow, ultimately dissipated by friction \citep[e.g.,][]{simmons1978life,randel1985observed,ma2021lorenz,cai2007dynamical}. However, study cases using limited-area energetics and Lorenz Energy Cycle (LEC) diagnostics indicate that barotropic conversions can be positive and contribute to cyclone intensification, implying that the mean-flow kinetic energy may directly feed transient eddies during development \citep[e.g.,][]{bulic2006limited,dias2011energy,black2013universal,cavicchia2018energetics}. This pathway appears to be relatively more relevant in weakly baroclinic environments and/or in hybrid–subtropical systems, where diabatic processes and enhanced barotropic conversions may jointly promote eddy growth \citep[e.g.,][]{veiga2008analysis,pezza2014large,cavicchia2019physically}. More recently, \citet{de2025lorenz} presented a large-sample, semi-Lagrangian LEC climatology for South Atlantic cyclones, showing that barotropic conversions are ubiquitous during cyclone evolution and typically peak near maturity, and that they can even exceed baroclinic conversions for some intense clusters.

In this study, we use cyclone energetics to objectively cluster extratropical cyclones forming over the southwestern South Atlantic into distinct groups with contrasting energetic pathways. Building on the semi-Lagrangian framework of \citet{de2025lorenz}, we diagnose the Lorenz Energy Cycle (LEC) for more than 3,800 cyclones with genesis in the main cyclogenesis regions adjacent to South America. To enable a consistent comparison across storms with different lifetimes, we phase-average the energy-cycle terms over objectively defined life-cycle stages \citep{couto2024new}. The resulting multivariate energetic descriptors are then clustered to identify recurrent energy patterns. Finally, we assess the occurrence of baroclinic and barotropic instability during cyclone intensification as an independent dynamical validation of the inferred energetic regimes.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Data}\label{sec:data}

The primary data source used in this study is the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) fifth-generation reanalysis, ERA5 \citep{hersbach2020era5}. This dataset has global coverage, with a horizontal grid spacing of approximately 31 km (0.25$^\circ$). It includes 137 vertical levels, ranging from near the Earth's surface (1000 hPa) to the top of the atmosphere (1 hPa), and provides hourly data. ERA5 contains estimates of several atmospheric variables; for the energy computations, we used geopotential, zonal and meridional wind, vertical velocity on isobaric surfaces, and temperature. ERA5 reanalysis data are produced using a state-of-the-art data assimilation system that incorporates a large number of observations, including satellite and in situ measurements \citep{hersbach2020era5}.

The cyclone tracks used in this study are the same as those in \citet{gramcianinov2020analysis, couto2024new, de2025lorenz}. The tracks are derived from the "Atlantic Extratropical Cyclone Tracks Database" \citep{gramcianinov2020data}, which identifies cyclones based on central relative vorticity at 850 hPa ($\zeta_{850}$). Cyclones are tracked using the TRACK algorithm \citep{hodges1994general, hodges1995feature} and the method outlined by \citet{hoskins2002new}, spanning the period 1979--2020. Here, as in \citet{de2025lorenz}, we considered only cyclones with genesis in the ARG, LA-PLATA, and SE-BR cyclogenesis regions (Figure \ref{fig:1_tracks_genesis_frequency}a). The cyclone selection criteria and processing are the same as those employed in \citet{couto2024new, de2025lorenz}; for further details, please refer to these references. These selection criteria resulted in 6789 cyclones with genesis in these regions: 4015 in ARG (59.1\%), 1288 in LA-PLATA (18.9\%), and 1486 in SE-BR (21.8\%).

\subsection{Life Cycle Detection}
\label{sec:life_cycle}

Cyclones have variable lifetimes, being able to last for less than 24 hours to over 10 days \citep[e.g.][]{trigo2006climatology,gramcianinov2019properties}. Therefore, to normalize their duration and enable comparisons of cyclone energetics, we averaged the energy diagnostics over four distinct life cycle phases: incipient, intensification, mature, and decay, similarly to \citep{de2025lorenz}. This was performed using the open-source Python package Cyclophaser, which identifies cyclone life cycle phases from the time series of relative vorticity at the system's central position and its first derivative \citep{de2025cyclophaser}. The cyclone life cycle phases are then identified from peaks and troughs in the vorticity time series. For a detailed description of the procedure, the spatial distribution of cyclone life cycle phases in the South Atlantic, and statistics such as duration, displacement, speed, and intensity for each phase, see \citet{couto2024new}. For standardizing the cyclone life cycle, we considered only cyclones with a complete life cycle in the standard sequence — incipient, intensification, mature, and decay — resulting in 3820 systems, approximately 56\% of the original sample. Figure \ref{fig:1_tracks_genesis_frequency} summarizes the resulting tracks used in this study, along with their seasonal occurrence.

\subsection{Lorenz Energy Cycle Computation}

The LEC framework employed here is the same as in \citet{de2025lorenz}, which used the open-source Python application LorenzCycleToolKit \citep{de2024lorenzcycletoolkit}. A semi-Lagrangian framework was used to compute the LEC for a $15^\circ \times 15^\circ$ computational domain centered on the cyclone's central position. The energy budget equations for the zonal and eddy forms of APE and kinetic energy are expressed as:


\begin{flalign}
\frac{\partial A_Z}{\partial t} &= BA_Z - C_Z - C_A + G_Z \\
\frac{\partial A_E}{\partial t} &= BA_E - C_E + C_A + G_E \\
\frac{\partial K_Z}{\partial t} &= BK_Z + C_Z - C_K + B\Phi_Z - D_Z \\
\frac{\partial K_E}{\partial t} &= BK_E + C_E + C_K + B\Phi_E - D_E 
\end{flalign}

In these equations, APE is divided into zonal ($A_Z$) and eddy ($A_E$) components, as is kinetic energy ($K_Z$ and $K_E$, respectively). $C_A$ represents the conversion between $A_Z$ and $A_E$, $C_E$ denotes the conversion from $A_E$ to $K_E$, $C_K$ signifies the transformation from $K_E$ to $K_Z$, and $C_Z$ describes the conversion from $A_Z$ to $K_Z$. The generation of APE and the dissipation of kinetic energy are indicated by $G$ and $D$, with $G_Z$ and $G_E$ denoting the generation of $A_Z$ and $A_E$, and $D_Z$ and $D_E$ representing the dissipation of $K_Z$ and $K_E$, respectively. The terms $BA_Z$, $BA_E$, $BK_Z$, and $BK_E$ represent the fluxes of zonal and eddy APE and kinetic energy across the boundaries. The terms $B\Phi_Z$ and $B\Phi_E$ are grouped with $C_Z$ and $C_E$, respectively, because both arise from the same derivation of the $K_Z$ and $K_E$ balances. \citet{muench1965dynamics} notes the difficulty in interpreting $B\Phi_Z$ and $B\Phi_E$, indicating that they are related to the downward flux of kinetic energy (i.e., toward lower altitudes) and represent the emergence of kinetic energy at the boundaries of the computational domain. For the full mathematical formulations of these processes, we refer to \citet{muench1965dynamics} and \citet{brennan1980zonal}.

As our goal was to evaluate the energy patterns of cyclonic development in the Southwestern Atlantic, we considered only a subset of terms for analysis: $A_E$, $K_E$, $C_A$, $C_K$, $BK_E$ $BA_E$ and $G_E$, which govern eddy (cyclone) development. This selection facilitates synthesis by focusing on terms relevant to eddy development and, because we applied PCA followed by k-means clustering, reduces the number of input features, enhancing interpretability and mitigating potential issues associated with highly collinear variables.

This term selection is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:2_20070643_lps_track_publication} using a diagram we refer to as the Lorenz Phase Space (LPS). As an example, we use cyclone ``20070643'' from the database, which exhibited the most negative (i.e., most intense) central relative vorticity during the 21st century. These diagrams display distinct LEC terms on the $x$- and $y$-axes; circle size scales with $K_E$, and circle color indicates $G_E$. We define two types of LPS: (1) a ``Conversion'' LPS (Fig.~\ref{fig:2_20070643_lps_track_publication}a), in which the $y$-axis shows $C_A$ and the $x$-axis shows $C_K$; and (2) an ``Import'' LPS (Fig.~\ref{fig:2_20070643_lps_track_publication}b), in which the $x$-axis represents $BK_E$ and the $y$-axis represents $BA_E$. The ``Conversion'' LPS highlights the relative importance of baroclinic and barotropic conversions, whereas the ``Import'' LPS indicates the relative importance of eddy-energy imports and exports. Plot annotations are included to aid interpretation.

The example system had genesis at 2007-07-24 14:00 UTC in the LA-PLATA region and lysis at 2007-07-29 03:00 UTC in the Southeastern Atlantic (Fig.~\ref{fig:2_20070643_lps_track_publication}c). The LPS diagrams (based on 6-hourly data) reveal that, from a conversion perspective (Fig.~\ref{fig:2_20070643_lps_track_publication}a), the system is initially driven by both barotropic and baroclinic processes. During early intensification, it increasingly feeds the zonal circulation via barotropic conversion ($K_E \rightarrow K_Z$). From late intensification through maturity and early decay, barotropic conversions dominate the energy cycle, and during late decay both $C_A$ and $C_K$ indicate energy transfer from the eddy to the zonal circulation. From the boundary-flux perspective (Fig.~\ref{fig:2_20070643_lps_track_publication}b), the system exhibits nearly neutral boundary fluxes of $A_E$ at genesis, while $K_E$ is imported. During early intensification, there is a net influx of both forms of eddy energy. Over the subsequent evolution, $BK_E$ tends to decrease and even indicates export prior to maturity. After maturity, although there are periods with export of both forms of eddy energy, the boundary fluxes are, overall, closer to neutral. There is also a large diabatic contribution ($G_E$) during early intensification, which decreases over time. Lastly, $K_E$ increases gradually from genesis to maturity and decreases rapidly afterward.


We also investigated the contribution of each subterm associated with $C_K$, as these provide a more detailed perspective on the dynamical processes governing barotropic conversions:


\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
C_K = \int_{p_t}^{p_b} \frac{1}{g}  & \Bigg( \underbrace{ \left[ \frac{\cos\phi}{a} (u)_{\lambda} (v)_{\lambda} \frac{\partial}{\partial\phi} \left(\frac{[u]_{\lambda}}{\cos\phi}\right) \right]_{\lambda\phi}}_{\text{(A)}} + \underbrace{\left[ \frac{(v)_{\lambda}^2}{a} \frac{\partial [v]_{\lambda}}{\partial\phi}\right]_{\lambda\phi}}_{\text{(B)}} + \underbrace{\left[ \frac{\tan\phi}{a} (u)_{\lambda}^2 [v]_{\lambda}\right]_{\lambda\phi}}_{\text{(C)}} \\
& + \underbrace{ \left[(\omega)_{\lambda}  (u)_{\lambda} \frac{\partial [u]_{\lambda}}{\partial p}\right]_{\lambda\phi}}_{\text{(D)}} + \underbrace{ \left[(\omega)_{\lambda}  (v)_{\lambda} \frac{\partial [v]_{\lambda}}{\partial p}\right]_{\lambda\phi}}_{\text{(E)}} \Bigg)  dp
\end{split}
\label{eq:CK}
\end{equation}

Where \(p_t\) and \(p_b\) are the top and bottom pressure levels used for the analysis (100 hPa and 1000 hPa, respectively), \(g\) is the gravitational acceleration, \(\phi\) is longitude, \(\lambda\) is latitude, \(a\) is the Earth’s radius, \(u\) and \(v\) are the zonal and meridional wind components, and \(\omega\) is the vertical velocity in isobaric coordinates (pressure, \(p\)). The \([X]_{\lambda}\) denotes the zonal mean, while \((X)\) represents eddy-related terms. The complete mathematical expressions for all LEC terms, including the eddy–mean decomposition, treatment of lateral fluxes, pressure–work terms, and an extended methodological discussion, are provided in \citet{de2025lorenz}.

The $C_K$ subterms can be interpreted as follows. Term (A) expresses the eddy momentum flux linked to the meridional (north--south) gradient of the zonal wind. Term (B) represents the meridional flux of kinetic energy associated with the meridional wind and its meridional gradient. Term (C) describes the meridional flux of zonal kinetic energy. Term (D) captures the zonal and vertical flux associated with the vertical shear of the zonal wind, whereas term (E) accounts for the meridional and vertical flux associated with the vertical shear of the meridional wind. Among these contributions, term (A) is the most directly connected to barotropic instability, since shear instabilities are fundamentally driven by horizontal wind shear \citep{rayleigh1895stability,kuo1949dynamic,chen1985note,mak1989local,yokota2012tropical,read2020baroclinic}. The remaining terms represent secondary contributions and/or modulate the primary instability, reflecting interactions between horizontal and vertical fluxes of kinetic energy.

\subsection{Clustering Analysis}

Here, we perform a cluster analysis for identifying the Energy Patterns (EP) related to cyclones in the Southwestern Atlantic. For this we used the main LEC terms related to eddy development ($A_E$, $K_E$, $C_A$, $C_K$, $BK_E$ $BA_E$ and $G_E$), as previously discussed. This analysis consisted of the three steps, which are described bellow: 1) principal component analysis (PCA) for reducing dimensionality 2) determining the best number of clusters using objective indices, 3) applying the clustering analysis.

Given that more than 3{,}800 cyclones were analysed in this study, we applied PCA to reduce the dimensionality of the LEC feature space prior to clustering. PCA constructs a set of mutually orthogonal principal components (PCs) as linear combinations of the original variables, ordered such that PC1 explains the largest fraction of the total variance, followed by PC2, and so on. Dimensionality reduction is achieved by retaining only the first $K$ PCs that explain most of the variability in the data. This step is widely adopted in meteorological pre-clustering workflows \citep[e.g.,][]{camus2014,de2022hybrid,borato2024evaluation,pozo2025characterising,zhong2025advanced}. The PCA was performed with the open-source Python library PyEOF \citep{zheng_2021}.

Let $N$ be the number of cyclones and $p=28$ the number of features. We organised the predictors into a matrix
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times p},
\end{equation}
whose columns are the LEC terms evaluated as phase-mean values over four lifecycle phases (incipient, intensification, mature, and decay). Specifically, for each term $n=1,\ldots,7$ and phase $\phi \in \{\mathrm{ic},\mathrm{it},\mathrm{ma},\mathrm{de}\}$, a feature $T_{n,\phi}$ is included, so that
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X} = \big[T_{1,\mathrm{ic}}, \ldots, T_{7,\mathrm{ic}},\;
               T_{1,\mathrm{it}}, \ldots, T_{7,\mathrm{it}},\;
               T_{1,\mathrm{ma}}, \ldots, T_{7,\mathrm{ma}},\;
               T_{1,\mathrm{de}}, \ldots, T_{7,\mathrm{de}}\big],
\end{equation}
yielding $7 \times 4 = 28$ features.

Prior to PCA, each feature (column) was standardised to have zero mean and unit variance using the \texttt{StandardScaler} implementation in scikit-learn \citep{pedregosa2011scikit}. Denoting by $x_{ij}$ the value for cyclone $i$ and feature $j$, we computed
\begin{align}
\mu_j &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} x_{ij},\\
s_j  &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N} (x_{ij}-\mu_j)^2},\\
z_{ij} &= \frac{x_{ij}-\mu_j}{s_j},
\end{align}
forming the standardised matrix $\mathbf{Z}=[z_{ij}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times p}$ on which PCA was applied. We retained the first $K=15$ PCs, which explain approximately $90\%$ of the cumulative variance (Figure \ref{fig:S1_pca_clustering_validation}a).

After dimensionality reduction with PCA, we clustered the cyclones using the $k$-means algorithm \citep{macqueen1967}. Owing to its simplicity, versatility, and low computational cost, $k$-means is one of the most widely used clustering methods in the geosciences, although other powerful alternatives exist \citep[e.g.,][]{fachieri2025introduccao}. Let $\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times K}$ denote the matrix of PCA scores for the $N$ cyclones retaining the first $K$ principal components, and let $\mathbf{y}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$ be the $i$-th row of $\mathbf{Y}$. The $k$-means method partitions the set of points $\{\mathbf{y}_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ into $C$ clusters by minimizing the within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS),

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{J} = \sum_{c=1}^{C}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_c}\left\lVert \mathbf{y}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_c \right\rVert^2,
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{I}_c$ is the index set of points assigned to cluster $c$ and $\boldsymbol{\mu}_c\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$ is the cluster centroid (i.e., the mean of the points in cluster $c$). The optimization is performed iteratively using Lloyd's algorithm: (i) assign each point to the nearest centroid in Euclidean distance, and (ii) update each centroid as the mean of the points currently assigned to it, repeating until convergence \citep{lloyd1982least}. Here, we used the scikit-learn implementation of $k$-means with $k$-means++ initialization \citep{arthur2006k}, which selects initial centroids in a distance-aware manner to improve convergence and reduce the chance of poor local minima.

Because $k$-means requires the number of clusters to be specified \emph{a priori}, we adopted an objective, multi-metric procedure to select this value. More than 100 cluster validity indices (CVIs) have been proposed in the literature \citep{arbelaitz2013extensive,hassan2024z}; here we combine five complementary CVIs that quantify cluster compactness and separation from different perspectives (Table~\ref{tab:cvi_summary}). For each candidate number of clusters $C$ (tested over a prescribed range), we computed all five indices from the $k$-means partition in the retained PCA space.

To place all CVIs on a common scale, each index $I_m(C)$ (with $m=1,\ldots,5$) was rescaled to $[0,1]$ using min--max normalization,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{I}_m(C) = \frac{I_m(C) - \min\limits_{C} I_m(C)}{\max\limits_{C} I_m(C) - \min\limits_{C} I_m(C)}.
\end{equation}
For indices where smaller values indicate better clustering (Davies--Bouldin), we inverted the normalized score as
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{I}_{\mathrm{DB}}^{\,*}(C) = 1 - \widetilde{I}_{\mathrm{DB}}(C).
\end{equation}
Finally, we defined a composite (unweighted) agreement score as the mean of the normalized CVIs,
\begin{equation}
\overline{I}(C) = \frac{1}{5}\sum_{m=1}^{5}\widetilde{I}_m^{\,*}(C),
\end{equation}
and selected the optimal number of clusters as the maximizer of $\overline{I}(C)$. This procedure consistently indicated $C=3$ as the optimal partition for the full dataset, supporting three distinct energy patterns while reducing reliance on any single validity criterion  (Figure \ref{fig:S1_pca_clustering_validation}b).

\subsection{Diagnositc fields}

To investigate the key physical processes driving cyclonic development, we performed a composite analysis centered on cyclone centers. The analysis is restricted to the intensification phase as a proxy for the mechanisms governing cyclone development. For each cyclone, the composite was constructed over a $30^{\circ} \times 30^{\circ}$ domain centered on the cyclone center at the central time step of the intensification phase. This domain allows us to examine both the dynamics within the LEC domain ($30^{\circ} \times 30^{\circ}$) and those in the surrounding environment, thereby enabling a distinction between the dynamical processes occurring inside and outside the domain. For cyclones whose intensification phase contained an odd number of time steps, the three central time steps were used, whereas for those with an even number of time steps, the two central time steps were used.

For the composite analysis, we also excluded systems whose intensification phase lasted less than 24 hours. This was done to exclude short-lived systems and to avoid contaminating the analysis with time steps too close to the incipient or mature stages, during which the energetics exhibit distinct behavior \citep{de2025lorenz} and, therefore, likely also the dynamical fields. After this filtering, the number of analyzed cyclones decreased from 3820 to 2733. For each energy pattern, the reduction was as follows: EP1, 332 cyclones (from 444); EP2, 776 cyclones (from 979); and EP3, 1625 cyclones (from 2397).


Potential vorticity (PV) is a useful diagnostic of cyclonic development because, for adiabatic and frictionless flow, it is approximately materially conserved and combines the effects of absolute vorticity and stratification in a single quantity \citep{hoskins1985use}. In this study, PV is computed using the hydrostatic Ertel formulation in pressure coordinates \citep{bluestein1993observations}:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PV}
=
-g\left(
\frac{\partial u}{\partial p}\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial y}
-
\frac{\partial v}{\partial p}\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial x}
+
(\zeta + f)\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial p}
\right),
\end{equation}

where \(u\) and \(v\) are the zonal and meridional wind components, respectively, \(\theta\) is the potential temperature, \(p\) is pressure, \(g\) is the gravitational acceleration, and \(f\) is the Coriolis parameter. The relative vorticity is defined as

\begin{equation}
\zeta = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}.
\end{equation}

All partial derivatives were evaluated using centered finite differences at interior grid points, while forward and backward one-sided finite differences were applied at the domain boundaries. The same numerical scheme was adopted for the computation of temperature advection (AdvT).

As a proxy for baroclinic instability, we used the maximum Eady growth rate (EGR), which estimates the growth rate of the most unstable baroclinic mode in the Eady model \citep{eady1949long,lindzen1980simple}. In its continuous form, EGR is given by

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EGR} = 0.31 \frac{|f|}{N} \left| \frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial z} \right|,
\end{equation}

where \(f\) is the Coriolis parameter, \(N\) is the Brunt--V\"ais\"al\"a frequency, \(z\) is height, and \(\mathbf{V}=(u,v)\) is the horizontal wind vector. The Brunt--V\"ais\"al\"a frequency is defined as

\begin{equation}
N = \left( \frac{g}{\theta} \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z} \right)^{1/2},
\end{equation}

where \(g\) is the gravitational acceleration and \(\theta\) is potential temperature.

As a compact diagnostic of the horizontal redistribution of upper-level kinetic energy around jet streaks and troughs, upper-level kinetic energy advection at 250 hPa was computed as

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{KE}_{\mathrm{adv}}
=
-\mathbf{V}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2}\lvert \mathbf{V}\rvert^2\right),
\end{equation}

where \(\mathbf{V}=(u,v)\) is the horizontal wind vector at 250 hPa. Positive values of \(\mathrm{KE}_{\mathrm{adv}}\) indicate local import of kinetic energy by horizontal advection, whereas negative values indicate local export.

To assess conditions favorable to barotropic instability, we used a local Rayleigh--Kuo-type diagnostic at 250 hPa \citep{rayleigh1895stability,kuo1949dynamic}:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RK}
=
\beta - \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2},
\end{equation}

where \(\beta = \partial f/\partial y\), \(u\) is the zonal wind component, and \(y\) is the meridional coordinate. In classical barotropic instability theory, for a parallel basic flow \(U(y)\), a necessary condition for instability is that \(\beta - \partial^2 U/\partial y^2\) changes sign somewhere within the domain. Here, \(\mathrm{RK}\) was evaluated locally from the 250 hPa zonal wind field as a proxy for regions where the meridional gradient of absolute vorticity may reverse sign, indicating an environment favorable to barotropic instability.


Ageostrophic geopotential flux convergence (AFC) was computed at 250 hPa as a diagnostic of downstream development and local energization of the disturbance \citep[e.g.][]{orlanski1991life,orlanski1993ageostrophic}:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AFC}
=
-\nabla \cdot \left( \mathbf{v}_{ag}' \, \Phi' \right),
\end{equation}

where \(\mathbf{v}_{ag}'\) is the anomalous ageostrophic wind and \(\Phi'\) is the geopotential anomaly. The anomalous ageostrophic wind was defined as

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{v}_{ag}' = \mathbf{v}' - \mathbf{v}_g',
\end{equation}

where \(\mathbf{v}'\) is the horizontal wind anomaly and \(\mathbf{v}_g'\) is the geostrophic wind anomaly derived from the geopotential anomaly. Positive AFC values indicate local convergence of anomalous ageostrophic geopotential fluxes, which is consistent with downstream development and the local accumulation of
eddy kinetic energy \citep[e.g.][]{orlanski1995stages,ndarana2021downstream,pinheiro2022contributions}.

In the original downstream-development framework, perturbations are commonly defined relative to a background mean flow or to a low-frequency component of the circulation \citep[e.g.][]{pinheiro2022contributions,pinheiro2024deepening}. Here, as an approximation suitable for the composite analysis, anomalies were defined as deviations from the 30-year monthly climatological mean for the corresponding calendar month. For events spanning two different months, the background state was obtained from a weighted mean of the two monthly climatologies in order to avoid artificial discontinuities.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

To investigate whether the energy patterns (EP1, EP2, and EP3) differ significantly in terms of LEC diagnostics and associated dynamical structure, we performed two complementary analyses: (i) inter-EP comparisons of scalar variables and (ii) association analysis between LEC terms and scalar descriptors derived from the dynamical fields. The statistical analysis was performed for all 24 LEC terms and for scalar features derived from the storm-centered dynamical fields used in the composite analysis. Rather than adopting a pointwise approach, each two-dimensional field was summarized into 13 scalar descriptors computed over the inner storm-centered domain. These included the spatial mean, the mean absolute value, the value at the cyclone center, cardinal-sector means (north, south, east, and west), border means, and north--south and east--west contrasts. This strategy was adopted to reduce the dimensionality of the composite fields while retaining physically interpretable measures of field intensity and structural asymmetry. In particular, the mean absolute value was included because the spatial mean of signed fields, such as temperature advection and ageostrophic flux convergence, may approach zero owing to sign cancellation. The use of cardinal sectors and zonal/meridional contrasts is also physically consistent with the LEC framework, which is based on zonal means and deviations from the domain mean.

For each scalar variable, normality was assessed independently within each EP group using the Shapiro--Wilk test \citep{shapiro1965analysis}. Homogeneity of variances was evaluated using the Brown--Forsythe modification of Levene's test \citep{brown1974robust}. In the present dataset, all tested variables departed from normality when evaluated within EP groups, and therefore all inter-EP comparisons were conducted using the Kruskal--Wallis rank-based test \citep{kruskal1952use}. When the global test was significant, pairwise comparisons were performed using Dunn's multiple-comparison procedure \citep{dunn1964multiple}, with Holm's sequential adjustment applied to control the familywise error rate \citep{holm1979simple}. Because a large number of variables were tested, the raw \emph{p}-values from the global Kruskal--Wallis tests were further adjusted within each analysis block using the Benjamini--Hochberg false discovery rate procedure.

To avoid overinterpreting statistical significance in a large-sample setting, effect sizes were reported alongside all global and pairwise comparisons. For the global inter-EP comparisons, we used \(\varepsilon^2\) as an effect-size measure for the Kruskal--Wallis test \citep{kruskal1952use}. Pairwise contrasts were summarized using the rank-biserial correlation coefficient. Following the workflow adopted in the analysis pipeline, variables were interpreted primarily on the basis of effect size and physical plausibility, rather than on adjusted \emph{p}-values alone. 

To assess the relationship between cyclone energetics and dynamical structure, we calculated Pearson's product--moment correlation coefficient \citep{pearson1920notes} and Spearman's rank correlation coefficient \citep{spearman1987proof} between the LEC terms and the scalar descriptors derived from the dynamical fields. Pearson's \(r\) was used as a measure of linear association, whereas Spearman's \(\rho\) was used to quantify monotonic association and was adopted as the primary interpretive metric because it is more robust to the non-Gaussian distributions typical of LEC terms and derived field features. Divergences between Pearson's \(r\) and Spearman's \(\rho\) were interpreted as indicative of non-linear but still monotonic relationships. 

We also assessed monotonic trends in annual cyclone counts (one value per year; 1979--2020) for each energy-pattern (EP) group using the Mann--Kendall (MK) test \citep{mann1945nonparametric,kendall1948rank}. To evaluate serial dependence, we applied the Ljung--Box portmanteau statistic $Q(h)$ to detrended residuals, using $h=\min(10,\lfloor n/5\rfloor)=8$ for $n=42$ years. Detrending was performed with the Theil--Sen slope estimator \citep{sen1968estimates}, and the residuals were then tested with the Ljung--Box procedure \citep{ljung1978measure}; no EP exhibited significant autocorrelation ($p\ge 0.05$). Accordingly, we report trend significance based on the original MK test. As a robustness check, we also computed and archived MK-based approaches that explicitly address serial correlation, including the Hamed--Rao variance correction, the Yue--Wang effective-sample-size correction, pre-whitening, and trend-free pre-whitening \citep{KulkarniVonStorch1995,hamed1998modified,YueEtAl2002_TFPW,YueWang2004}; all approaches yielded consistent conclusions (not shown). Trend magnitude and the corresponding 95\% confidence interval were estimated using the Theil--Sen slope and are reported in cyclones~yr$^{-1}$.


\section{Results and Discussion}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{General energetic behavior of Southwestern Atlantic cyclones}

Figure \ref{fig:3_phase_density_2x2} contains the conversion and imports LPS diagrams for all cyclones considered in the current study, averaged for each cyclone lifecyle phase. On then, the bins indicate mean $G_E$ values and the contours, cyclone density. The cyclone density always increase towards the graph origin and decreases outwards, with the regions of higher density changing for each develoment phase. This indicates that the cyclones behavior represent a continuum, a feature ubiquoustly noted by all previous studies attempting an objective cyclone classification \citet[e.g.][]{hart2003cyclone,yanase2014parameter,graf2017objective,dekker2018characteristics,corner2025classification}.

For the conversion LPS, there is a tendency for systems to concentrate in the upper-right quadrant across all phases (Fig.~\ref{fig:3_phase_density_2x2}a,c,e,g). This indicates a shared importance of baroclinic and barotropic processes for eddy development, as previously noted by \citet{de2025lorenz}. This concentration strengthens from the incipient to the intensification phase and then gradually weakens. The $G_E$ term follows a similar evolution: although values are averaged within each $3 \times 3$ bin, an increase in the relative contribution of $G_E$ from incipient to intensification can be identified, followed by a decrease afterward. Also, $G_E$ increase approximately with increasing $C_A$. A smaller fraction of cases falls in regimes in which barotropic processes alone govern cyclone development (lower-left quadrants) or in which neither baroclinic nor barotropic conversions favor eddy growth (lower-right quadrants). For cyclones in the lower-left quadrant, $G_E$ contributions are generally weaker. Cyclones in the lower-right quadrant (which are rare, accounting for roughly 5\%) are either driven by $G_E$ or by energy imports.

The ``import'' LPS exhibits greater dispersion than the ``conversion'' LPS. Although cyclone behavior spans a continuum, some phase-dependent preferences emerge. In the incipient phase (Fig.~\ref{fig:3_phase_density_2x2}b), there is a concentration in the right half-plane, indicating a preference for imports of $A_E$ during this phase, whereas $K_E$ may be either exported or imported. During intensification (Fig.~\ref{fig:3_phase_density_2x2}d), the relative importance of the left half-plane increases, particularly the upper-left quadrant, indicating a higher tendency to export $A_E$ while importing $K_E$. During maturity (Fig.~\ref{fig:3_phase_density_2x2}f), there is a stronger concentration in the lower-left quadrant, indicating exports of both forms of eddy energy. Lastly, during decay (Fig.~\ref{fig:3_phase_density_2x2}h), cyclones concentrate in either the lower-left or upper-right quadrants, suggesting that, in some cases, energy export contributes to cyclone dissipation, whereas in others, energy import may help maintain the system despite frictional processes \citep{de2025lorenz} and/or enhanced conversion back to the mean flow.

\subsection{Energy patterns and key energy pathways for cyclone development}
\label{sec:energy_patterns}

The clustering analysis yielded three centroids defining the energy patterns (EPs) of cyclones with genesis in the southwestern Atlantic. Pairwise effect sizes based on the rank-biserial correlation coefficient indicate a clear energetic separation among the three EPs, with non-negligible differences for most LEC terms in all pairwise contrasts (Figure~\ref{fig:S2_pairwise_effectsize_lec_terms}). Notably, most EP1--EP2 comparisons already show meaningful effect sizes, although the strongest separations are generally associated with contrasts involving EP3. Non-negligible differences are also evident for several terms not directly included in the clustering procedure, supporting the robustness of the segmentation and indicating that the EPs capture broader and physically consistent distinctions in cyclone energetics.

Figure~\ref{fig:4_lps_combined}a shows the conversion LPS for these EPs, indicating that, for all EPs, the energy cycle is largely modulated by barotropic and baroclinic conversions that feed the eddy. For EP3, these contributions are more modest, including the contribution from $G_E$. For EP2, systems begin the incipient phase with moderate conversions, which increase nearly proportionally during intensification, return approximately to their initial values, and then decrease toward near-neutral conditions. EP1 also begins with modest conversions, but exhibits large increases during intensification and further increases during the mature phase, particularly in barotropic conversion. EP1 also shows relatively large conversion values during decay. $G_E$ is highest during intensification for both EP1 and EP2, but $K_E$ is larger for EP2 during intensification, suggesting that EP1 systems tend to intensify more slowly.

The $C_A$ and $C_K$ terms are now assessed only during the intensification phase, when these terms reach their largest magnitudes. In terms of $C_A$ magnitude, EP1 and EP2 are statistically similar (Fig.~\ref{fig:S2_pairwise_effectsize_lec_terms}), with values peaking near the surface, remaining relatively large from low to mid-levels, and increasing again between 600 and 300\,hPa (Fig.~\ref{fig:S3_vertical_levels}a). In contrast, EP1 exhibits significantly larger $C_K$ (Figure~\ref{fig:4_lps_combined}a); therefore, we further investigate the characteristics of this term for EP1 systems. For EP1, $C_K$ reaches its largest magnitudes between 400 and 250\,hPa (Fig.~\ref{fig:5_ck_subterms_vertical_profiles}a). This vertical structure is broadly mirrored by EP2 cyclones, whereas EP3 cyclones show consistently low $C_K$ values throughout the atmosphere (Fig.~\ref{fig:S3_vertical_levels}b).

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:5_ck_subterms_vertical_profiles}b, the $C_K$ subterm with the largest median magnitude is term E, which is related to the vertical flux of eddy meridional momentum coupled with the vertical shear of the mean meridional wind. This is followed by term B, associated with the meridional flux of eddy kinetic energy and the meridional gradient of the mean meridional wind, and term A, which represents the primary barotropic instability mechanism. Terms D and C exhibit low median magnitudes and are mostly positive, indicating that they contribute to energy transfer from the eddy to the mean flow. Term D is associated with the vertical flux of eddy zonal momentum coupled with the vertical shear of the mean zonal wind, whereas term C represents eddy zonal kinetic energy modulated by the mean meridional flow. Although term E has a larger median magnitude than term B, the latter exhibits greater variability and is therefore more intense in some systems. Among EP1 cyclones, term E is dominant in 43\% of the cases, followed by term B in 38\% and term A in 19\%.

Overall, these results indicate that EP3 comprises weaker, day-to-day systems, with genesis predominantly in ARG. These cyclones have a less active energy cycle and occur preferentially during the austral warm season. Although they exhibit substantial interannual variability, no statistically significant trend is detected in their occurrence. In contrast, EP2 represents relatively intense systems, with genesis primarily in LA-PLATA and ARG, weak seasonality, and energetics characterized by modest baroclinic conversions and diabatic contributions, together with especially large imports of $A_E$ and $K_E$ during early development. EP2 is the only EP exhibiting a statistically significant trend, with an overall increase.

Finally, EP1, while being the least frequent and most active during the cold season, shows the strongest conversions throughout the life cycle, particularly barotropic conversion, with relevant contributions from diabatic processes. However, these eddy-energy sources are partly offset by energy export, which becomes stronger during the mature and decay phases. EP1 does not show a marked interannual variability or a statistically significant trend.


\subsection{Cyclone structure}\label{sec:composites}

Figure \ref{fig:8_dynamical_composites_epall_relative} presents the dynamical composites for EP1, EP2, and EP3. The first row (\ref{fig:8_dynamical_composites_epall_relative}a--c) shows upper-level PV ($PV'_{200}$), lower- to mid-tropospheric baroclinicity (represented by $EGR_{850-500}'$), and sea-level pressure. For PV and EGR, anomalies are shown with respect to the total cyclone composite (EPALL), whereas for SLP the total composite is shown. For all EPs, the SLP field exhibits a clear cyclonic signature, marked by closed isobars. For both EP2 and EP3 there is a ridge downstream of the surface cyclone. However, for EP1 the ridge seems to be upstream of the surface cyclone, a feature which will be relevant for future analysis. 

The upper-level PV anomaly patterns differ substantially among the EPs, indicating distinct large-scale dynamical configurations. For EP1, the $PV'_{200}$ field exhibits an elongated structure that resembles a PV-streamer-like signature, which is consistent with an equatorward intrusion of high stratospheric PV \citep[e.g.,][]{hoskins1985use,hu2004variations,wernli2007identification}. Because this is an EPALL-relative anomaly field, this pattern should be interpreted as streamer-like rather than as a definitive PV streamer. In this case, there is no equally compact or collocated cyclonic signature in the lower-tropospheric PV anomaly ($PV'_{850}$; \ref{fig:8_dynamical_composites_epall_relative}d), suggesting weaker vertical coupling between the upper- and lower-level anomalies. 

In contrast, EP2 displays a more compact $PV'_{200}$ structure, with a cutoff-like or strongly wrapped trough--ridge dipole, which appears more clearly coupled to the lower-level PV anomaly ($PV'_{850}$; Fig. \ref{fig:8_dynamical_composites_epall_relative}e). This configuration is consistent with a more favorable vertical coupling between upper- and lower-tropospheric PV anomalies, which may support more efficient cyclone intensification than in the more elongated EP1 configuration \citep{davis1991potential,huo1999interaction,schemm2019efficiency}. EP3 presents a upper and low-level $PV'$ signal inverted than EP2, indicating a weaker  dynamical coupling and thus, weaker development.

The $EGR_{850-500}'$ fields are also markedly distinct among the EPs. For EP1, enhanced baroclinicity is found around the cyclone center and extends downstream of it. In contrast, for EP2, the baroclinic zone is elongated in a NW--SE direction, similarly to the configuration described by \citet{crespo2021potential} for cyclones with genesis in the Uruguay (LA-PLATA) region. This suggests that these systems develop within a strongly baroclinic environment embedded in the South Atlantic storm-track region \citep{hoskins1990existence,chang2002storm,hoskins2005new}. The observed pattern further suggests differences in the relative positioning between the cyclone core and the surrounding low-level baroclinic reservoir in EP1 and EP2. In both cases, however, the upper-level PV anomalies appear to interact with a favorable baroclinic environment, supporting ascent and cyclonic intensification \citep{davis1991potential,huo1999interaction,besson2021global,crespo2021potential}. Lastly, EP3 cyclones exhibit a weaker relative baroclinic signature in the same region where EP2 shows its maximum $EGR_{850-500}$ anomaly.
 

Figure \ref{fig:8_dynamical_composites_epall_relative}d-f presents low-level PV ($PV'_{850}$), temperature advection ($AdvT'_{850}$) and wind vectors at 850 hPa - all anomalies in respect to EPALL. In respect to $PV'_{850}$, as previously discussed, EP1 presents a less spatially coherent field, while EP2 presents a cyclonic PV anomaly coupled to the upper-level disturbance. EP3 presents a weak anticyclonic anomaly near the cyclonic center, indicative of a weaker cyclonic development.

EP2 presents a clear cold--warm dipole embedded in a cyclonic circulation, consistent with the canonical frontal structure of extratropical cyclones \citep[e.g.][]{dacre2025kinematic}. In this sense, the pattern agrees with South Atlantic cyclone composites associated with organized frontal development \citep{gramcianinov2019properties}. Warm advection in the cyclone warm sector is, according to quasigeostrophic theory, associated with ascent along and ahead of the warm front \citep{davies2015quasigeostrophic}. However, the resulting positive low-level PV anomalies are more directly linked to the diabatic processes accompanying this ascent, particularly condensation and latent heating, rather than to thermal advection alone \citep{hoskins1985use,huo1999interaction,attinger2021systematic}.

EP1, in turn, is dominated by relative cold-advection anomalies, together with an anticyclonic circulation southwest of the surface cyclone, suggestive of a post-frontal environment. In combination with the streamer-like upper-level PV structure, this configuration is consistent with a post-frontal or secondary-development setting, although it is not by itself sufficient to diagnose secondary cyclogenesis. A similar South Atlantic configuration was described by \citet{iwabe2009event}, while \citet{gramcianinov2019properties} also suggested that some cyclones forming near southeastern Brazil and the southeastern South Atlantic are associated with secondary development. Lastly, EP3 exhibits an anomaly pattern opposite to EP2, indicating a weaker or displaced canonical frontal thermal-advection dipole relative to the EPALL composite. In this sense, EP3 does not appear to display the same degree of organized frontal thermal contrast found in EP2.

Figure \ref{fig:8_dynamical_composites_epall_relative}g--i presents anomalies of upper-level ageostrophic geopotential flux convergence (AFC$_{250}'$) and kinetic energy advection ($\mathrm{AdvKE'}_{250}$), together with hatching indicating regions where the meridional gradient of absolute vorticity changes sign, that is, where the Rayleigh--Kuo (RK) necessary condition for barotropic instability is satisfied. The RK hatching is shown for the total EP composite rather than for the anomalous fields. Since the Rayleigh--Kuo criterion provides only a necessary, not sufficient, condition for barotropic instability, the hatching should be interpreted as background dynamical context rather than as direct evidence of barotropic growth.

The AFC$_{250}'$ field highlights important distinctions between EP1 and EP2. EP2 presents a dipolar pattern, with relatively intense positive anomalies on the western side of the cyclone and relatively intense negative anomalies on its eastern side. This indicates enhanced local upper-level eddy kinetic energy accumulation to the west of the cyclone core and depletion to the east, relative to EPALL, a configuration consistent with an organized downstream-dispersive upper-level energetics pattern during cyclone intensification \citep{orlanski1993ageostrophic,orlanski1995stages,piva2010energetics,pinheiro2022contributions}. The upper-level wind anomalies are consistent with this interpretation, as EP2 exhibits a more coherent cyclonic-anomalous circulation around the cyclone core, with stronger meridional flow anomalies on the flanks of the system, suggesting a more sharply defined shear and deformation environment. The $\mathrm{AdvKE'}_{250}$ anomalies are broadly consistent with this interpretation, since EP2 also exhibits a dipolar structure, suggesting a more organized redistribution of kinetic energy along the upper-level flow, with positive anomalies downstream of the surface cyclone.

In contrast, EP1 exhibits a broader positive AFC$_{250}'$ anomaly over and downstream of the cyclone center, while negative anomalies are found mainly upstream and to the southwest. Relative to EPALL, this suggests a less canonical downstream-dispersive AFC pattern than in EP2, with comparatively greater local upper-level energy accumulation in the vicinity of and downstream of the cyclone core. In this sense, the downstream development signature appears less clearly organized in EP1, suggesting that other energetic pathways may play a relatively larger role in sustaining cyclone growth. For EP1, the surface cyclone appears anomalously closer to the upper-level trough axis, that is, downstream of an upstream ridge and less horizontally displaced from the upper-level PV anomaly than in EP2. This suggests a more trough-controlled upper-level configuration, consistent with upper-level-induced cyclogenesis or with systems developing beneath a pre-existing upper-level trough, rather than with the more canonical downstream-dispersive pattern inferred for EP2 \citep{schultz2019extratropical,sinclair2000classification,graf2017objective}.
 The negative $\mathrm{AdvKE'}_{250}$ anomalies reinforce the interpretation that kinetic energy redistribution in EP1 differs from that of EP2, although this term should be viewed primarily as a redistribution term rather than as a direct source or sink of cyclone intensification.

EP3, in turn, is characterized by predominantly negative AFC$_{250}'$ anomalies around the cyclone core, indicating weaker upper-level energetic support relative to EPALL. Its upper-level wind anomalies are weaker and more diffuse than in EP2, which is consistent with the less organized AFC pattern. Finally, all EPs exhibit RK sign reversals close to the cyclone center, suggesting that the necessary condition for barotropic instability is locally satisfied in all of them. This supports the interpretation that barotropic processes may be present in the three EPs, as a more localized phenomenon near the upper-level shear zone \citep[e.g.][]{mak1989local,wernli1998effect,cai2004local,riviere2006role}.

\subsection{Relationship between energy terms and dynamical fields}\label{sec:correlation}

The question of whether the LEC terms are connected to the dynamical fields presented in Sect.~\ref{sec:composites} naturally arises. Here, we evaluate whether linear relationships exist between the LEC terms and scalar quantities derived from the dynamical fields for all cyclones considered in this study, using Pearson correlation coefficients ($r$). The monotonicity of each association was verified using
Spearman's rank correlation coefficient ($\rho$); sign agreement between Pearson $r$ and Spearman $\rho$ was found for 100\% of the 126 pairs with $|r| \geq 0.20$, and only two pairs showed a modest magnitude divergence ($|\,|r| - |\rho|\,| > 0.10$). 

Although correlation does not necessarily imply causality, it can provide supporting evidence when the association is physically plausible. This is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:9_pearson_epall_by_field_type}, where only values with $|r| > 0.2$ are displayed. Although this threshold is usually considered to indicate a weak relationship, the large variability in the cyclone dataset and the complexity of LEC terms (which often involve multiple contributions, including departures from the zonal mean, deviations from the domain mean, and different forms of spatial averaging) make these
associations noteworthy. Given the sample size ($n \approx 2733$), the critical value for statistical significance at $p < 0.05$ (two-tailed Pearson test) is $|r_{\mathrm{crit}}| \approx 0.037$; consequently, all displayed associations ($|r| \geq 0.20$) are highly significant ($p < 10^{-21}$ in the worst case). It must be noted, however, that statistical significance does not preclude the possibility of spurious associations in a large sample: physical plausibility and sign consistency with Spearman's $\rho$ are therefore used as primary interpretive filters. The $\mathrm{PV}_{850}$ field yielded only 7 of 91 possible pairs (7.7\%) above the display threshold, and therefore is not accessed. Similarly, the scalar fields derived from RK does not present any meaningful physical meaning and are also not accessed.

\begin{figure}[ht!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{9_pearson_epall_by_field_type.png}
    \caption{Pearson correlation matrix between scalar variables derived from the dynamical fields used in the composite analysis and the LEC terms. Only correlations with $|r| > 0.2$ are shown. Hatches indicate combinations where $|\,|r| - |\rho|\,| > 0.10$}
    \label{fig:9_pearson_epall_by_field_type}
\end{figure}

For the $\mathrm{AdvT}_{850}$ field (Fig.~\ref{fig:9_pearson_epall_by_field_type}a), the highest correlations are found for LEC terms related to APE, as expected from quasigeostrophic theory. For $C_a$, the strongest correlations are concentrated in the western sector ($r = -0.46$, $\rho = -0.55$) and the northern sector ($r = -0.44$, $\rho = -0.55$), alongside the domain mean ($r = -0.44$). The negative sign is physically consistent with the above structure: stronger cold advection in the western and northern sectors (more negative $\mathrm{AdvT}$) indicates a more vigorous cold front and a steeper meridional temperature gradient, which supports stronger baroclinic conversion \citep{lorenz1955available}. A notable divergence between Pearson and Spearman for $C_a \times \mathrm{sector\_N}$ ($|r| = 0.44$ vs $|\rho| = 0.55$) suggests that the $C_a$–temperature advection relationship has a moderately non-linear component, physically consistent with the intensification of frontal zones on the equatorward side of extratropical cyclones, where frontogenesis, thermal advection, and warm--moist pre-frontal transport may accompany or favor rapid cyclone deepening \citep{dacre2025kinematic}.

Significant correlations are also found for $BA_E$, the dominant signal being net $A_E$ import through the eastern boundary: eastern border ($r = +0.28$), eastern sector ($r = +0.28$), and E–W contrast ($r = +0.33$). The western border and sector show negative correlations ($r = -0.24$ and $-0.37$, respectively). The physical interpretation is straightforward: warm advection on the eastern side increases $BA_e$ (Ae import through the eastern boundary), whereas cold advection on the western side decreases $BA_e$ (Ae export through the western boundary). Consequently, $BA_E > 0$ requires precisely the warm-east/cold-west asymmetry that defines the frontal structure of SH cyclones; the E–W contrast correlation captures both effects simultaneously. Finally, relatively weak but significant correlations involving $K_E$ ($|r| = 0.22$–$0.35$) are likely indirect: $C_A$ and $BA_e$ increase the $A_E$ reservoir, which is subsequently converted into $K_E$ through $C_E$. The marginal correlation for $C_K$ with the domain mean ($r = +0.20$) is noted in the next paragraph.

For the $\mathrm{AFC}_{250}$ field (Fig.~\ref{fig:9_pearson_epall_by_field_type}b),
the highest correlations are found for the reservoir terms $K_E$ and $A_E$, both associated with the amplitude of AFC ($|\mathrm{AFC}|$ domain mean; $r_{K_E} = +0.53$, $r_{A_E} = +0.51$). The positive sign indicates that stronger upper-level ageostrophic geopotential flux convergence (in absolute value, hence capturing both positive convergence and divergence events) is associated with larger eddy energy reservoirs. This is physically consistent with the downstream development framework of \citet{orlanski1991life,orlanski1993ageostrophic}: positive AFC at the upper tropospheric level reflects local accumulation of eddy kinetic energy by ageostrophic energy import, while negative AFC indicates downstream dispersal. The large absolute-value correlation, rather than the signed mean, implies that the key signal is the intensity of the upper-level energy redistribution, regardless of its direction, rather than a systematic bias toward convergence or divergence. This is in accordance with the AFC composites (Figure \ref{fig:8_dynamical_composites_epall_relative}g--i), as EP1 and EP2, which represent the most intense systems, present higher energy dispersion and convergence, respectively.

Regarding the signed domain-mean AFC, $C_A$ shows $r = +0.43$ and $C_k$ shows
$r = -0.22$. The positive $C_A$–AFC relation suggests that,
on average, cyclones with more organized upper-level downstream energy redistribution tend to have stronger baroclinic and barotropic conversions. For $C_A$, positive sigifficant correlations are found also for the magnitude of domain mean, central value and all sectors. Meanwhile, for $C_K$ it is found only for the eastern flank. This suggests that barotropically dominant cyclones (EP1) are associated with a broader, more symmetric AFC pattern, rather than the predominantly eastward-dispersing AFC signature typical of classically
baroclinic downstream development \citep{orlanski1991life}, as noted in the composite analysis for EP2.

The spatial pattern of AFC correlations with $BA_E$ and $BK_E$ are also noteworthy. The correlation sign is reversed for sectorial and border values. For $BA_E$ positive values on western border and sector are found and negative for the eastern border and sector. This is indicates that the symmetric pattern of ageostrophic convergence on the west and divergence on the east is related with APE imports, while the reverse is apparent for $BK_E$.

For the $\mathrm{KE\_adv}_{250}$ field, high individual correlation is found for $A_E$  ($r = +0.43$), but relevant values are also found for $K_E$ ($r = +0.32$) and $C_A$ ($r = +0.43$). Similarly as for AFC, both are associated with the amplitude of $\mathrm{|KE\_adv}_{250}|$ domain mean, reinforcing the interpretation that the overall upper-level energy redistribution efficiency correlates with the baroclinic energy pathway.
The correlation signals for $\mathrm{AdvKE}_{250}$ borders and sectors are largely opposite of those found by AFC.
The highest individual correlation is $A_e \times \mathrm{sector\_W}$ ($r = -0.45$, $\rho = -0.45$), which is in accordance with EP2 (more intense) and EP3 (less intense) composites (Figure \ref{fig:8_dynamical_composites_epall_relative}h,i), displaying dominantly negative and positive $\mathrm{AdvKE'}_{250}$ values on the cyclone western sector, respectively. Notably, $C_k$ shows positive correlations with several KE advection features, consistent with cyclones with the strongest barotropic instability (EP1) being characterized by large eddy-energy export or weaker imports (Figure \ref{fig:4_lps_combined}). For $BK_E$, the only correlation above the threshold is with the western border ($r = +0.37$), suggesting that KE advection from the west directly modulates the lateral $K_E$ import.

For the $\mathrm{PV}_{200}$ field (Fig.~\ref{fig:9_pearson_epall_by_field_type}d), strong correlations are found for The E–W contrast (east $-$ west) of $G_E$ ($r = +0.59$, $\rho = +0.57$), $A_E$ ($r = +0.57$, $\rho = +0.59$), and $C_A$ ($r = +0.45$, $\rho = +0.47$). This implies that a larger contrast — that is, a stronger cyclonic upper trough to the west combined with a more pronounced anticyclonic ridge to the east — is associated with larger diabatic generation, eddy APE, and baroclinic conversion. For $G_E$, the physical mechanism likely involves the PV-induced ascending motion beneath the upper trough: by invertibility \citep{hoskins1985use}, the negative PV anomaly to the west drives asymmetric mid-tropospheric lifting, concentrated near the axis of maximum temperature gradient between the trough and the downstream ridge. This lifting sustains precipitation and latent heat release, thereby increasing $G_E$. For $C_A$ and $A_E$, the strong negative correlations with the western border
($r_{C_A} = -0.30$, $r_{A_E} = -0.44$) and western sector ($r_{C_A} = -0.24$, $r_{A_E} = -0.37$) indicate that a stronger cyclonic PV anomaly to the west (more negative $\mathrm{PV}_{200}$) is associated with larger baroclinic conversion and eddy APE. This is consistent with the PV-coupling mechanism for baroclinic development \citep{hoskins1985use,davis1991potential}.

\section{Summary and Conclusions}

- LPS se mostrou uma ferramenta útil para visualização dos principais termos do LEC relacionados ao desenvolvimento dos ciclones, de forma a condensar a informação e apresentar de um modo intuitivo em comparação ao tradicional diagrama do LEC com as quatro caixas e setas de conversão. Não que vá substitui-lo, mas para análise de sistemas transientes é uma boa opção.

-  While conversion LPS shows that the difference among systems is more represented by the instensity of the Ck and Ca conversions, the imports LPS shows distinct pathways.

- EP1 high conversion on the decay phase phase act to maintain the system against energy dissipation \citep{de2025lorenz} and exports.

- EP2: classic baroclinic systems, with strong AFC convergence; EP1: possibly secondary cyclogenesis, with weakened AFC, and strong barotropic conversions. But all systems presented both baroclinic and baroclinic conversions. Ibc was expected, but the dominant role of Ibt is somewhat unexpcted and localized. 

- Importação de AE pode estar relacionado com transporte remoto de calor, enquanto que importação de KE pode ser o downstream development - mais estudos são necessários.

- A analise de correlação indicou que o LEC no framework semi-lagrnagiano é capaz de representar campos dinamicos fisicamente realistas e trazer novos insights sobre o desenvolvimento desses sistemas.
